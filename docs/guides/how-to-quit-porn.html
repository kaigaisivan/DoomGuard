How to quit porn

If you are reading this, you have probably tried to stop before, or you are tired of how it makes you feel. This guide is practical and has no judgement. Quitting is easier when you set things up so willpower is not the only thing holding the line.

Start with your reason

Write down, in your own words, why you want to stop. Maybe it is your relationship, your focus, your sleep, your sense of self-respect, or your faith. A clear reason is what you will come back to on a bad night. Keep it somewhere you will see it.

Steps that make it more realistic

1. Learn your triggers

Most people find that it happens in a pattern: late at night, alone, after a stressful day, when bored, or when they are avoiding something. Note the time, place and feeling for a week. You cannot avoid a trigger you have not noticed.

2. Make access harder

Do not depend on being strong at 1 a.m. Block adult sites on your phone and computer, remove accounts and saved bookmarks, and turn off anything that suggests videos. 3. Change where and when you use your phone

Many people slip in bed or in the bathroom. Charge your phone outside the bedroom, use an alarm clock, and keep screens in shared spaces in the evening. If a certain app leads you there, limit or remove it.

4. Ride out the urge

Urges rise, peak and fade, and they usually fade faster than they feel like they will. When one hits, stand up, change rooms, and do something physical for ten or fifteen minutes: pushups, a walk, a cold shower, calling someone. Do not negotiate with the urge. Just move.

5. Build something to replace it

The habit filled a gap: boredom, stress, loneliness, or a need to switch off. Decide what will fill it. Exercise, hobbies, time with friends, prayer or meditation, learning a skill. Have two or three options ready.

6. Look after the basics

Poor sleep, long stretches of isolation and constant stress make urges stronger. Regular sleep, daily movement and real conversations help more than they sound like they should.

7. Get accountability

Telling a trusted friend, partner or mentor changes the situation: the habit stops being a secret. You can agree to check in once a week. If you cannot imagine telling anyone, that is a good sign of how much secrecy is part of the problem.

8. Plan for a slip

Many people slip at least once. What matters is what happens next. Shame tends to make the next slip more likely, not less. After a slip, write down what led to it, adjust one thing in your setup, and carry on. A single slip is information, not proof that you failed.

When to ask for professional help. If you feel unable to stop despite real attempts, if it is damaging your relationships, work or finances, or if it comes with depression, anxiety or thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor, counsellor or therapist. They have helped many people with this and will not be shocked. This guide is general information, not medical advice.
